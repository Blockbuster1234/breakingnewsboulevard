Iran deal gives Trump a way out of war - but at a high cost

2026-04-08

**Iran deal gives Trump a way out of war – but at a high cost** Washington, 3 November 2024 – A tentative agreement to revive the 2015 Joint Comprehensive Plan of Action (JCPOA) could provide former President Donald Trump with a diplomatic exit from the looming prospect of a military confrontation with Iran, analysts say, though the price of such a deal may prove politically and economically steep. The JCPOA, which placed strict limits on Iran’s nuclear programme in exchange for the lifting of UN, US and EU sanctions, collapsed after Trump withdrew the United States in 2018 and reimposed a sweeping sanctions regime. Since then, Tehran has gradually expanded its uranium enrichment activities, bringing the country closer to the threshold of weapons‑grade material. In recent months, heightened rhetoric from both Washington and Tehran – including naval deployments in the Persian Gulf and a series of covert strikes attributed to Iranian proxies – has raised fears of an inadvertent escalation that could spiral into open conflict. Against this backdrop, back‑channel talks facilitated by the European Union and mediated by Oman have produced a draft framework that would see Iran curb its enrichment to 20 % purity – well below the 90 % needed for a nuclear bomb – in return for a phased removal of secondary sanctions targeting Iran’s oil, banking and shipping sectors. The proposal also includes a limited restoration of access to the Society for Worldwide Interbank Financial Telecommunication (SWIFT) system for humanitarian trade, and a commitment from Washington to refrain from imposing new unilateral sanctions for a period of two years. For Trump, who has repeatedly criticised the JCPOA as a “disaster” and promised to keep Iran “in check” through maximal pressure, the outline offers a face‑saving route to avoid a war that could jeopardise his 2024 presidential campaign. “A deal that prevents Iran from acquiring a nuclear weapon while keeping American troops out of another Middle‑East quagmire is exactly the kind of outcome the American people deserve,” said a senior aide speaking on condition of anonymity. The aide added that the former president views the agreement as a way to demonstrate strength through diplomacy rather than costly military entanglement. Nevertheless, the prospective accord carries significant drawbacks. Economically, lifting secondary sanctions would unlock an estimated US$50 billion–$70 billion in Iranian oil revenue over the next eighteen months, according to the International Energy Agency. Critics argue that such an influx could bolster Iran’s regional influence, funding proxy groups in Yemen, Syria and Lebanon, and potentially undermine US allies such as Israel
